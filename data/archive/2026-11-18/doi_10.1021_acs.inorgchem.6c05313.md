## 摘要
<jats:title>Abstract</jats:title>
                  <jats:p>Optical properties of luminescent materials are usually affected by dopant concentration. In this work, ultrabroadband NIR-II emission and vivid purple body color are simultaneously obtained in LiSc1–xCrx(WO4)2 (x = 0–1.0) compounds with high Cr3+ concentration. At a high optimal concentration of x = 0.20, the corresponding PL spectrum upon 520 nm excitation presents a NIR broadband with a FWHM of 241 nm. Density functional theory calculations provide an understanding of the geometric, electronic, and optical properties of the LiSc(WO4)2:Cr3+ compound, explaining the intense visible absorption and NIR-II broadband emission. By varying the Cr3+ concentration, the peak wavelength of the broadband NIR emission can be tuned from 1060 to 960 nm, with a narrowing of FWHM from 241 to 210 nm. The color of the LiSc(WO4)2:Cr3+ samples changes from light purple through purple to dark red with increasing Cr3+ content, which is attributed to the intensification of Cr–O charge transfer and d–d transitions. Furthermore, the optimized pigment showed excellent chromatic robustness against heat, humidity, acid, and alkali corrosion. These findings establish a clear structure–property relationship linking activator doping concentration to optical performance, indicating its significant potential for practical use as an ultrabroadband NIR-II phosphor and next-generation purple pigment.</jats:p>
