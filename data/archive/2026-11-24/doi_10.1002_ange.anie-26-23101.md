                    Organic small‐molecule photothermal materials with second near‐infrared (NIR‐II) absorption have attracted enormous interest for biomedical applications, yet simultaneously achieving strong NIR‐II absorption and high photothermal conversion efficiency remains a formidable challenge. Herein, we report to conduct the heteroatom‐modulated aggregation of diketopyrrolopyrrole (DPP) derivatives (named as DAQ‐T and DAQ‐Fu) by single‐atom substitution to construct high‐performance NIR‐II‐absorbing photothermal agents. Single‐crystal x‐ray diffraction and density functional theory calculations reveal that oxygen‐for‐sulfur substitution precisely modulates electrostatic potential distributions and tailors intermolecular π–π interactions by leveraging the distinct electronegativities of chalcogens, driving a transformation from
                    <jats:italic>H</jats:italic>
                    ‐aggregation in DAQ‐T to
                    <jats:italic>J</jats:italic>
                    ‐aggregation in DAQ‐Fu. The resulting
                    <jats:italic>J</jats:italic>
                    ‐aggregated DAQ‐Fu nanoparticles feature a distinct absorption band at 1064 nm with a molar extinction coefficient of 3.26 × 10
                    <jats:sup>4</jats:sup>
                    L mol
                    <jats:sup>−1</jats:sup>
                    cm
                    <jats:sup>−1</jats:sup>
                    , and exhibit an outstanding photothermal conversion efficiency of 92.4%. This photothermal effect exerts potent antitumor efficacy by regulating apoptosis and immune‐related signaling pathways, enabling the synergy of photothermal therapy and as‐induced immunogenic cell death to suppress both primary and distant tumors growth with about 90% and 63% inhibition rates, respectively, under biocompatible irradiation. This work demonstrates atom‐precise aggregation modulation, offering a promising pathway to engineer NIR‐II‐absorbing organic small‐molecule photothermal agents for synergistic photothermal cancer immunotherapy.
