## 摘要
Theranostic metal-organic frameworks (MOFs) have emerged as promising nanoplatforms for cancer and other disease treatment, and their combination with second near-infrared (NIR-II) fluorescence has opened many functional possibilities for simultaneous imaging and therapy. However, synthesizing NIR-II fluorescent MOFs that combine high brightness with strong absorbance remains a significant challenge, primarily because achieving efficient light harvesting without compromising structural stability is difficult. Herein, we report a rationally engineered neodymium-based metal-organic framework (Nd-MOF) that integrates NIR-II fluorescence imaging and photothermal therapy within a single architecture. By exploiting coordinated electronic interactions between lanthanide centers and organic ligands, the framework exhibits enhanced light-harvesting capability, as reflected in a high molar extinction coefficient of 9.86 × 10-3 mL/g·cm, alongside efficient photothermal conversion, achieving 21% under NIR irradiation. The PVP-coated Nd-MOF (PMF) achieves a quantum yield of 2.48% in the NIR-II region, enabling high-contrast fluorescence imaging with minimal background interference. The resulting nanoplatform demonstrates efficient NIR-II-imaging-guided photothermal tumor ablation, as validated by systematic in vitro and in vivo studies, with high therapeutic efficacy, minimal off-target toxicity, and good biocompatibility. This work establishes a rigorous strategy for constructing multifunctional lanthanide-MOF-based systems for advanced cancer therapy.
