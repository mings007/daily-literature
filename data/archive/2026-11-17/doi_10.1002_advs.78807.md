## 摘要
<jats:title>ABSTRACT</jats:title>
                  <jats:p>
                    Methicillin‐resistant
                    <jats:italic>Staphylococcus aureus</jats:italic>
                    (MRSA) pneumonia represents a critical threat owing to its high mortality and antibiotic resistance. Near‐infrared II (NIR‐II) fluorescent photothermal therapy (PTT) enables deep‐tissue heating but risks incomplete bacterial clearance and thermal damage. Symmetric donor‐acceptor‐donor (D‐A‐D) NIR‐II molecules often suffer from high molecular weights, low fluorescence, and suboptimal photothermal conversion. Herein, we report asymmetric D‐A‐π‐D NIR‐II aggregation‐induced emission luminogens (AIEgens) featuring a benzothiopyrylium acceptor, triphenylamine donors, and furan or thiophene as π‐bridge. Furan strengthens non‐covalent conformation locking while weakening intermolecular
                    <jats:italic>π–π</jats:italic>
                    stacking, thereby enhancing both fluorescence and photothermal performance. AIEgen 4fop achieves an optimal photothermal conversion efficiency of 84.3% and a NIR‐II photoluminescence quantum yield of 2.31% in toluene. To boost antibacterial activity, 4fop was co‐encapsulated with the thermally responsive NO donor BNN6 into Pluronic F‐127 nanoparticles. In a murine pneumonia model, this nanoplatform substantially reduces bacterial burden, suppresses inflammation through TNF pathway modulation, and promotes lung repair. This work establishes a rational D‐A‐π‐D design strategy and an integrated nanoplatform for the precise treatment of deep‐seated drug‐resistant infections.
