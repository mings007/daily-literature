## 摘要
<jats:title>Abstract</jats:title>
                  <jats:p>Second near-infrared (NIR-II)-responsive nanocomposite hydrogels have emerged as promising platforms for remotely controlled drug delivery. Advances in nanomaterial engineering and dynamic hydrogel design have enabled precise, externally regulated, and multifunctional control over therapeutic delivery. This article highlights representative photothermal materials (i.e., metal-based, carbon-based, hybrid, and emerging nanomaterials) used in the construction of NIR-II-responsive nanocomposite hydrogels. The rational nanomaterial design and hydrogel network engineering that synergistically regulate photothermal conversion and on-demand drug release have been emphasized. Current challenges and future opportunities are also discussed to inform the continued development of NIR-II-responsive hydrogel systems and their clinical translation for precision medicine.</jats:p>
