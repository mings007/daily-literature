## 摘要
<jats:title>ABSTRACT</jats:title>
                  <jats:p>Accurate identification of metastatic lymph nodes (LNs) remains a major challenge in esophageal squamous cell carcinoma (ESCC) surgery. Single‐cell RNA sequencing and multiplex immunohistochemistry reveal enrichment of fibroblast growth factor receptor 2 (FGFR2) in metastatic LNs, providing a clinically informed target for molecular imaging. Guided by this finding, an FGFR2‐targeted dual‐modality probe, 18F‐NOTA‐(ICG)‐DcP5, is engineered by integrating the cyclic peptide DcP5, indocyanine green (ICG), and a NOTA radiolabeling module within a unified molecular construct. The probe enables complementary positron emission tomography (PET) and second near‐infrared‐window (NIR‐II) fluorescence imaging across whole‐body and local operative settings, while competitive blocking supports an FGFR2‐dependent targeting component. Across subcutaneous and orthotopic ESCC models, a proof‐of‐concept lymph node dissemination model, and metastatic LN‐derived patient‐derived xenografts, the imaging strategy supports lesion detection, lymph‐node discrimination, and NIR‐II‐guided intraoperative localization. The probe achieves a NIR‐II tumor‐to‐background ratio of 3.64 ± 0.23, exceeding clinical ICG (1.39 ± 0.20) by 162%. A rapid ex vivo incubation protocol further distinguishes metastatic from non‐metastatic LNs in freshly resected human surgical specimens using histopathology as the reference standard (AUC = 0.9167). By bridging clinically informed target discovery with multimodal image‐guided intervention, this work advances a precision strategy for selective lymph ‐node management in ESCC.</jats:p>
