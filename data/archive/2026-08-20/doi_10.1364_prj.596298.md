## 摘要
<jats:p>
                    The second near-infrared (NIR-II, 900–1880 nm) window offers superior fluorescence imaging penetration and contrast. However, its clinical application remains constrained by the lack of U.S. Food and Drug Administration (FDA)-approved NIR-II fluorophores, leaving indocyanine green (ICG) as the only clinically approved contrast agent despite its suboptimal performance in the NIR-II window. Although optical lock-in detection (OLID) is effective in suppressing noise and background, its application has remained narrowly applicable because existing implementations require fluorophores with tailored excitation or polarization sensitivities—capabilities lacking in most clinically relevant probes—thereby impeding widespread clinical translation. This work presents a probe-general NIR-II
                    <jats:italic toggle="yes">in vivo</jats:italic>
                    lock-in fluorescence imaging technique that suppresses noise to improve the signal-to-background ratio (SBR) and signal-to-noise ratio (SNR) by up to 2500- and 300-fold, respectively. The enhancement doubles penetration depth, reduces contrast agent dosage or excitation intensity by 95%, enables tumor visualization using FDA-approved ICG with an SNR far exceeding the Rose criterion, and improves the spatiotemporal resolution of vascular imaging. This advancement of lock-in fluorescence imaging offers a promising solution for clinical applications, particularly in NIR-II surgical navigation.
                  </jats:p>
