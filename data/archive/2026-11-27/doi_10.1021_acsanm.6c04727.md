## 摘要
<jats:title>Abstract</jats:title>
                  <jats:p>Allergic rhinitis (AR) is an allergic inflammatory condition driven by immunoglobulin E (IgE) mediated type I hypersensitivity. Upon re-exposure to allergen, mast cells release histamine, which activates the histamine H1 receptor (H1R), leading to cytokine release, recruitment of inflammatory cells, and accumulation of reactive oxygen species (ROS). This cascade amplifies local inflammation and oxidative stress. Levocetirizine dihydrochloride (LCD), a second-generation antihistamine, is commonly used due to its high selectivity for H1R and low systemic toxicity. However, effective visualization of inflamed nasal lesions and targeted delivery of antihistamines to pathological nasal tissues remain challenging. To overcome these challenges, we designed an imaging-guided theranostic nano-delivery system (AL NPs) based on second near-infrared (NIR-II, 1000−1700 nm) fluorescence imaging. Silver sulfide nanoparticles (A NPs) were synthesized through biomineralization to produce stable NIR-II emission, enabling precise imaging and localization of inflamed areas in the nasal cavity. LCD was loaded onto the nanoparticles to counteract H1R-mediated acute reactions, reducing histamine-induced increases in vascular permeability, mucosal swelling, and local oxidative stress. This approach enables the rapid and preferential accumulation of LCD-loaded AL NPs in inflamed nasal tissues following intravenous administration, thereby facilitating NIR-II fluorescence-guided lesion visualization and localized therapy for AR.</jats:p>
